# Human review and appeal procedure

## Review
1. The investigator requests review once analyses are complete. An independent reviewer must be assigned, and that reviewer cannot be the case creator.
2. The reviewer examines the evidence, every method result and its limitations, the automated assessment (including missing and failed methods, and contradictions), and any process evidence.
3. The reviewer records an assessment, a rationale of at least 20 characters, and whether they agree with the automated outcome. Disagreement is recorded explicitly. The decision is hashed and chained into the audit trail.
4. A reviewer may instead request further analysis (the case returns to analysis) or record an inconclusive result.
5. A report can be finalised only after a review decision, and only by someone other than its author.

## Language
Use evidence-describing language: "consistent with", "inconclusive", "verification succeeded for the supported provenance record". Do not write "100% AI-generated", "definitively human-written", "criminally manipulated" or any other statement of certainty.

## Decisions affecting people
Treat an AEGIS outcome as one input. Before any adverse decision, obtain independent corroboration (process evidence, interviews, original sources). Give the affected person an opportunity to respond.

## Appeal
1. Anyone with the appeal permission can file an appeal or correction request, optionally on behalf of an affected party. Use a role or reference rather than personal details.
2. A reviewer other than the appellant moves it to under review, then records upheld, corrected or rejected with a resolution.
3. A correction creates a new report version that supersedes the previous one. The original remains preserved.
4. Reopening a finalised case requires the reopen permission and a recorded reason.
